With $M_{\max} \approx 1.4$, Weisshorn crosses the \emph{transonic} range (roughly $0.8 < M < 1.2$,
where subsonic and supersonic regions coexist around the body) and flies a few seconds in the low
supersonic range, so the nose is designed for this whole range rather than for one Mach number.
The 3U CubeSat ($100\times100\times\SI{340.5}{mm}$) sits in the nose bay, so the profile decides
how much of it fits inside the curved part. Carbon fibre blocks radio signals, so if the nose has
to be the RF window its shell must be glass fibre.

\subsection{What is assumed}

Each assumption is chosen so that the comparison between nose shapes is fair; none of them changes
the ranking.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Trajectory.} Point mass (thrust, drag and gravity; the rotation of the stable vehicle
is ignored), rail \SI{12}{m} at $84^\circ$, ISA, no wind, constant \SI{4.5}{kN} for \SI{9.1}{s}.
Propellant from $I_t/(I_{sp}g_0) = \SI{20.9}{kg}$, i.e.\ burn-out mass \SI{54.1}{kg}. The drag of
everything except the nose, the least certain part of the model at this stage, is scaled by a
single factor $k$ so that the baseline nose reaches the LVO apogee of \SI{9.0}{km}
(Section~\ref{sec:flight}). This gives $M_{\max}=1.47$, consistent with the LVO's Mach~1.4. All
noses share the same $k$, and Section~\ref{sec:robust} shows that the ranking does not change when
the rest of the drag is varied.
\item \textbf{Thermal envelope.} The LVO masses ($75 \rightarrow \SI{45}{kg}$) on a
\SI{30}{\celsius} day give $M_{\max} \approx 1.56$, the hottest credible flight. Heating is
checked on this case.
\item \textbf{Fins} (not yet defined for Weisshorn): four trapezoidal fins, root \SI{300}{mm},
tip \SI{120}{mm}, span \SI{160}{mm}, sweep \SI{180}{mm}, \SI{5}{mm} hexagonal section. They only
enter the common drag and the CP shift.
\item \textbf{Payload envelope.} A 3U unit needs an outer radius $r \geq \SI{77}{mm}$:
\SI{70.7}{mm} half-diagonal, \SI{3}{mm} clearance, \SI{2}{mm} guide, \SI{1.2}{mm} shell.
\item \textbf{Shell.} \SI{1.2}{mm} CFRP at the Firehorn as-built areal density
(\SI{0.98}{kg/m^2}), plus a \SI{60}{g} machined aluminium tip.
\item \textbf{Transonic nose wave drag} ($0.8 < M < 1.1$): smooth rise from zero at $M = 0.8$
to the computed $M = 1.1$ value at $M = 1.0$ (Section~\ref{sec:transonic}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What could not be found}
\label{sec:flags}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Alex Marseille's nose cone report} is not on the ERT Drive or the wiki. His
documented work is the CFD of the \SI{54}{mm} supersonic semester rocket; as CFD advisor he set
the semester goal ``WS (Cd, Nosecone)'' (2027\_C\_FD\_TM\_3), which is this study. The prior work
used instead is the 2021 supersonic nosecone semester project, the Wildhorn optimisation note
(2022\_FD\_TN\_C01) and the Firehorn nosecone DJF/DDF.
\item \textbf{No Weisshorn nosecone requirement} (length, mass, thermal) exists yet; the Weisshorn
nosecone pages are empty templates.
\item \textbf{Weisshorn fins, CG and mass breakdown} are not defined. The static margin is
therefore given as a \emph{change} relative to the baseline nose.
\item \textbf{No supersonic flight data} exists at ERT to calibrate drag or heating against.
\end{itemize}
