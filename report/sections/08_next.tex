\section{Open Points and Next Steps}
\label{sec:next}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Fluent verification (CFD team).} Run the selected geometry in the team's Fluent
workflow (density-based, k-$\omega$ SST, ideal gas, Sutherland, far field 10--20$D$ upstream and
radially, 30--50$D$ downstream) at $M = 0.95$, 1.2 and 1.47. This replaces the transonic
assumption of Section~\ref{sec:transonic} and checks the Euler wave drag with viscous effects.
A density-based solver solves the compressible equations in coupled form and is the usual choice
for flows with shocks. Sutherland's law gives the viscosity of air as a function of temperature.
The far-field distances keep the boundaries clear of the bow shock and the wake, so that the
imposed free-stream conditions do not disturb the solution.
The Euler wall-pressure files (\texttt{results/cp/}) are the reference to compare against.
\item \textbf{RocketPy update (simulation team).} Replace the LVS nose in the RocketPy model by
the final geometry and its $C_D(M)$ (\texttt{results/euler\_results.csv}), then re-check apogee,
static margin (2--4 cal, 2027\_C\_SE\_FD\_REQ\_07) and the \SI{30}{m/s} rail exit speed once the
fins are defined.
\item \textbf{Requirements to create} (none exists for the Weisshorn nosecone). Proposed:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
\item profile: Von K\'arm\'an, $L = \FINL{}$ ($L/D=4.5$, \FINLPHYS{} physical with the tip), coordinates from \texttt{nose\_profile\_final.csv}, radius tolerance $\pm\SI{0.5}{mm}$;
\item nosecone bay length (curved part + the cylinder that completes the 3U housing) \FINBAY{}, to be booked in the \SI{5.4}{m} LV budget (2027\_C\_SE\_REQ\_04);
\item thermal: withstand \SI{430}{K} on the outer surface for \SI{10}{s} (envelope stagnation temperature, an upper bound for the whole surface), replacing Firehorn's \SI{390}{K}/\SI{5}{s};
\item tip: metallic (Al 6082-T6), spherical radius \SI{4.5}{mm}, replaceable.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Material and RF.} If the payload or avionics need GNSS/telemetry through the nose,
the shell must be GFRP (2027\_C\_SE\_AV\_INTERFACE\_ST\_REQ\_07 asks for a $360^\circ$ RF window
$\geq 2D$ long). The nose is \SI{810}{mm} long, so it can be that window. GFRP has the same epoxy
temperature limit but about 40\,\% more mass for the same stiffness; the shape result does not
change. Glass fibre is an electrical insulator and lets radio waves through, whereas the
conductive carbon fibres screen them.
\item \textbf{Thermal test.} Update 2025\_C\_ST\_NOSECONE\_TEMPERATURE\_TSP to the new level and add a
shoulder/coupler bond specimen. The DP490 bond is the weakest point in the envelope case
(Section~\ref{sec:heating}).
\end{enumerate}
