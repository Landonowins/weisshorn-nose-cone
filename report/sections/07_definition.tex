The profile length $L$ is the length of the mathematical profile up to its theoretical apex.
The \SI{4.5}{mm} sphere is tangent to the profile at $x = $ \FINXT{} from that apex. Replacing the
apex by the sphere removes \FINTRUNC{} of length, hence the \FINLPHYS{} physical curved length.
The coordinates are in \texttt{results/nose\_profile\_final.csv} (200 points, $x$ from the tip,
clustered at the tip). Two notes for Structure:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Mould length.} The profile is longer than one print bed, so the PET-CF male mould
needs 2--3 segments. Joints should sit where the curvature is low, i.e.\ in the aft half.
\item \textbf{Tolerance.} $\pm\SI{0.5}{mm}$ on the radius moves $C_{D,\rm w}$ by much less than
1\,\%. A step at the tip/shell joint matters more: keep it below \SI{0.2}{mm} and forward-facing
steps at zero. A forward-facing step acts as a small blunt face in the supersonic flow: it
creates a local shock with a local pressure and heating peak, and it can trip the boundary layer.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Manufacturing plan}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Mould.} Male mould printed in PET-CF from the STL (Firehorn process). The \SI{870}{mm}
part (curved length + shoulder) is printed in three segments, joined at $x \approx 330$ and
\SI{600}{mm}, where the radius changes slowly. The segments are aligned on a central rod,
sanded, and sealed with release agent.
\item \textbf{Layup.} Prepreg $[0/45/0]_{SO}$ (\SI{1.2}{mm}), the Firehorn laminate. Plies are cut
from flat patterns and stop at $x \approx \SI{60}{mm}$, where the radius is already
about \SI{17}{mm}. This avoids the ply pile-up at the apex reported on Firehorn: as the circumference shrinks
towards the apex, the fabric can no longer lie flat and the plies overlap. Two extra
local plies are added over the last \SI{100}{mm} of the curved part and the shoulder, where the
coupler is bonded and the shell is hottest at the bond.
\item \textbf{Tip.} Machined Al 6082-T6 insert covering the first \SI{80}{mm}, threaded onto an Al
ring that is bonded inside the shell at the step $x = \SI{60}{mm}$ (Matterhorn/Firehorn
concept). The outer surface is flush with the shell within \SI{0.2}{mm}.
\item \textbf{Coupler.} Firehorn male glued coupler, scaled to \SI{180}{mm}, bonded on the
\SI{60}{mm} shoulder (2027\_C\_ST\_COUPLERS).
\item \textbf{Finish.} Sand and paint. Check the profile with a printed template (or a CMM) at
five stations against \texttt{nose\_profile\_final.csv}, tolerance $\pm\SI{0.5}{mm}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{CAD deliverables}

The folder \texttt{results/cad/} contains watertight binary STL solids (units mm, $x$ from the
tip, closed by a flat base at the end of the shoulder) for the seven candidates at $L/D = 4.5$
and for the final design with its \SI{4.5}{mm} tip
(\texttt{nose\_vonkarman\_LD4.5\_rn4.5mm\_FINAL.stl}). They are checked to be closed manifolds
whose volume matches the analytical volume to 0.05\,\%. The same files can be imported directly
into SpaceClaim for the Fluent runs of R6.
