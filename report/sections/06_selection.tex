\section{Selection}
\label{sec:selection}

\subsection{Profile}

Table~\ref{tab:matrix} scores the profiles at $L/D = 4.5$ from 1 (worst) to 5 (best) with weights 4:2:2:1
(performance 0.44, integration 0.22, manufacture 0.22, stability 0.11). Performance (apogee), integration
(3U length inside) and stability (static-margin change) are scored linearly between the worst and the best
design; manufacture is a judgement score (tangent shoulder and standard mould 5, kink penalised, cone 3).
Only hardware properties are scored; flight heritage is not a criterion.

\textbf{Von K\'arm\'an is selected.} Lowest wave drag at every computed Mach number, \SI{18}{m} above
power $\tfrac34$ and \SI{22}{m} above power $\tfrac12$, first in all seven robustness scenarios; ogive,
parabolic and LV-Haack lose \SIrange{69}{79}{m}, the cone \SI{132}{m}. Against the power noses it also wins
on integration: tangent shoulder (against $3.2^\circ$ and $4.8^\circ$ kinks) and more of the 3U inside
(\FINTHREEU{}, against \SI{217}{mm} and \SI{152}{mm}). Its advantage holds at $M \geq 1.2$, so it does not
hinge on the transonic model.

The outcome does not depend on the weights: with equal weights Von K\'arm\'an leads LV-Haack (3.95
against 3.48), with performance and integration swapped it is still first (4.10 against 4.09). LV-Haack
wins only if payload room carries more than about half the weight, i.e.\ if the payload grows beyond one
3U (Table~\ref{tab:alternatives}).

\subsection{Length}

Every \SI{90}{mm} ($\Delta L/D = 0.5$) around $L/D = 4$--4.5 is worth \TRADELEN{} of apogee (about
\SI{0.4}{kg} of mass margin) for about \SI{60}{mm} of nose bay and \SI{30}{g} of shell, with diminishing
returns ($L/D = 5$ would add only about \SI{39}{m}). With the LV capped at \SI{5.4}{m}
(2027\_C\_SE\_REQ\_04) and dry-mass growth expected (2027\_C\_SE\_REQ\_06), the design point is
\textbf{$L/D = 4.5$, $L = \SI{810}{mm}$}: \TRADELEN{} for \SI{62}{mm} of bay. If those \SI{62}{mm} cannot be
released, the same profile at $L/D = 4$ (\SI{720}{mm}, the LVS length) is the fallback.

\subsection{Tip}

A spherical tip of radius $r_n = \SI{4.5}{mm}$ ($r_n/R = 0.05$): it replaces the sharp apex that cannot be
made and chips on landing, for \BLUNTCOST{} of wave drag (\BLUNTAPOA{} of apogee, against \BLUNTAPOB{} for a
\SI{9}{mm} tip). The tip material is left to ST.
