\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

The chain is: Euler wave drag of each nose $\rightarrow$ vehicle drag build-up $\rightarrow$ ascent
trajectory $\rightarrow$ apogee, heating, stability. The wave drag is the only shape-dependent
quantity; the flight model converts it into apogee and also accounts for the length and mass of
each nose. The implementation is described in Appendix~\ref{app:scripts}.

\subsection{Nose wave drag: axisymmetric Euler solver}
\label{sec:euler}

Above Mach 1 the nose pressure drag is set by the inviscid outer flow (shock and expansion), so the
axisymmetric Euler equations (the Navier--Stokes equations without viscous terms) are solved for
each nose followed by a $5R$ cylinder. The boundary layer is thin at these Reynolds numbers (about
$2\times10^7$ on the nose length at $M_{\max}$); its main effect, skin friction, is added in the
flight model (Section~\ref{sec:flight}). In finite-volume form, per radian of azimuth,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial (\boldsymbol U V)}{\partial t} + \sum_{\rm faces} \boldsymbol F\cdot\boldsymbol n\, \ell\, r_f
= \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & p\,A & 0 \end{pmatrix}^{\!\top},
\qquad V = A\, r_c ,
\label{eq:euler}
\end{equation}
with $\boldsymbol U = (\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E)$ the conserved variables, $\boldsymbol F$ their flux
through a face of outward normal $\boldsymbol n$, length $\ell$ and mid-radius $r_f$, and $V = A r_c$
the volume per radian of a ring-shaped cell of area $A$ and centroid radius $r_c$. The source
$p\,A$ is the net radial pressure force on the two non-parallel azimuthal faces of the cell;
writing the volume as $A r_c$ preserves a uniform flow exactly.

The scheme is a cell-centred finite-volume method with HLLC approximate Riemann fluxes (shocks
resolved within two to three cells without oscillations), MUSCL reconstruction of the primitive
variables with the minmod limiter (second order away from shocks), and SSP-RK2 time integration with
local time stepping at CFL 0.85, since only the steady state matters. The body-fitted grid
($160\times80$ cells) starts on the stagnation streamline upstream of the tip and clusters points at
the tip and at the shoulder. The outer boundary lies outside the bow shock with characteristic
(non-reflecting) far-field conditions; the wall is a slip wall and the axis a symmetry line. The
drag coefficient is the integral of the wall overpressure over the nose,
\begin{equation}
C_{D,\rm w} = \frac{1}{q_\infty \pi R^2}\int_{\rm nose} (p-p_\infty)\,(-n_x)\, 2\pi r\,\mathrm ds ,
\label{eq:cdw}
\end{equation}
where $n_x$ is the axial component of the outward wall normal (negative on a forward-facing
surface). A run is converged when $C_{D,\rm w}$ changes by less than $2\times10^{-5}$ (relative)
over 250 iterations. The Mach numbers are computed from high to low (1.6, 1.4, 1.2, 1.1; also 1.3
for the cone and ogive), each started from the previous solution. The $M=1.1$ runs, with a large
subsonic region behind the bow shock, were stopped at 6000 iterations; they only set the end point
of the transonic model and carry an uncertainty of 1--2\,\%. The trade study interpolates
$C_{D,\rm w}(L/D)^2$, nearly constant by Equation~\eqref{eq:fineness}, between $L/D = 3$, 4 and 5.
All seven shapes, and the blunted Von K\'arm\'an nose, were also computed directly at $L/D = 4.5$
for $M = 1.6$, 1.4 and 1.2. The interpolation agrees with these direct runs within 0.3\,\%, but
scaling the parabolic and power-$\tfrac34$ noses from $L/D = 4$ alone underestimated their drag by
2--4\,\%, so the direct values are used at $L/D = 4.5$ throughout.

\textbf{Validation.} For a sharp cone the flow between shock and body is conical, and the Euler
equations reduce to the Taylor--Maccoll ordinary differential equation, integrated here to high
accuracy. The surface pressure is uniform, so $C_{D,\rm w} = C_p$. Figure~\ref{fig:validation}a
compares the two for four cone angles ($7.1^\circ$ to $20^\circ$, $M = 1.1$--2): the largest error
is 0.46\,\% (the $20^\circ$ cone at $M=1.2$ has a detached shock and no conical solution). The grid
convergence study (Figure~\ref{fig:validation}b) uses the Von K\'arm\'an nose at $L/D=4$ on three
grids ($80\times40$, $160\times80$ production, $320\times160$). At $M=1.4$ the observed order is
$p = 2.0$, the design order of the scheme, and the grid convergence index (GCI, Roache, safety
factor 1.25) of the production grid is 0.5\,\%; at $M=1.2$ the two-grid estimate ($p=2$, safety
factor 3) gives 1.1\,\%. The production grid is therefore accurate to about 0.5--1\,\%, well below
the 3--46\,\% by which the other shapes exceed the Von K\'arm\'an wave drag at $L/D = 4.5$.

\subsection{Transonic range}
\label{sec:transonic}

Steady Euler solutions converge too slowly below $M=1.1$, and near $M = 1$ the shocks interact with
the boundary layer, so viscous (RANS) computations are needed there anyway. The nose wave drag is
therefore modelled, for all shapes, as a smooth cubic rise from zero at $M=0.8$ (first sonic flow on
a slender nose) to the computed $M=1.1$ value at $M=1.0$, flat up to $M=1.1$. The Fluent runs of
Section~\ref{sec:next} replace it; until then its onset and amplitude are varied in the robustness
study (Section~\ref{sec:robust}).

\subsection{Flight model}
\label{sec:flight}

A point-mass trajectory is integrated from the \SI{12}{m} rail ($84^\circ$) to apogee. Along the
flight path, with $V_\infty$ the speed and $\gamma_f$ the flight-path angle,
\begin{equation}
m\frac{\mathrm dV_\infty}{\mathrm dt} = F_{\rm th} - \tfrac12\rho V_\infty^2 A_b C_D - m g\sin\gamma_f ,
\qquad
V_\infty\frac{\mathrm d\gamma_f}{\mathrm dt} = -g\cos\gamma_f ,
\label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
with the mass $m$ decreasing linearly during the burn and $\rho$ from the ISA. The drag coefficient
(reference area $\pi D^2/4$) is built up from components,
\begin{equation}
C_D = k\left(C_{D,f} + C_{D,\rm fins} + C_{D,\rm base} + C_{D,e}\right) + C_{D,\rm w}(M):
\label{eq:buildup}
\end{equation}
turbulent, roughness-limited skin friction on the wetted area of nose and body (Schlichting, with
a compressibility correction), fin pressure drag (blunt leading edges subsonic, Ackeret thin-airfoil
theory for the hexagonal section supersonic), the OpenRocket base-drag law (nozzle exit area
removed while burning) and the ERT-Sim excrescence law. Only $C_{D,\rm w}$ changes from one nose to
the next; a longer nose also adds wetted area to $C_{D,f}$ and shell mass to $m$. The factor $k$ is
set so that the baseline nose (Von K\'arm\'an, \SI{720}{mm}) reaches the LVO apogee of
\SI{9.0}{km}; the resulting $M_{\max}=1.47$ is close to the LVO's Mach 1.4 without further tuning.

\subsection{Heating}
\label{sec:heating_method}

No wall can exceed the stagnation temperature $T_0=T_\infty(1+\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2)$, the
temperature air reaches when brought to rest adiabatically, which is the Firehorn design rule. On
the hot day at $M = 1.56$, $T_0 = 288\times(1+0.2\times1.56^2) = \SI{428}{K}$ (\SI{155}{\celsius}).
How close a surface gets to it depends on the heat it receives, $q = h\,(T_r - T_w)$, and the heat
it conducts away. At the tip, the laminar stagnation-point coefficient (Fay--Riddell form without
dissociation, modified-Newtonian velocity gradient) is
\begin{equation}
h_s = 0.763\,\mathrm{Pr}^{-0.6}\sqrt{\rho_s\mu_s \left.\frac{\mathrm du_e}{\mathrm dx}\right|_s}\;c_p,
\qquad
\left.\frac{\mathrm du_e}{\mathrm dx}\right|_s = \frac{1}{r_n}\sqrt{\frac{2(p_s-p_\infty)}{\rho_s}} ,
\label{eq:hs}
\end{equation}
with the stagnation-point density, viscosity and pressure $\rho_s$, $\mu_s$, $p_s$ behind the normal
shock. Hence $h_s \propto r_n^{-1/2}$: a larger tip radius gives a thicker boundary layer and less
heating. Along the shell, the turbulent flat-plate correlation
$\mathrm{St}=0.0296\,\mathrm{Re}_x^{*-0.2}\mathrm{Pr}^{-2/3}$ (properties at Eckert's reference
temperature) drives the wall towards the recovery temperature
$T_r = T_\infty(1+r\tfrac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2)$, $r=\mathrm{Pr}^{1/3} \approx 0.89$, the temperature of
an insulated wall. The shell is modelled by 1D transient conduction through its thickness with an
adiabatic inner face (conservative: no radiation or internal convection), and a metal tip as a
single lumped mass heated through its wetted surface. Both are evaluated along the nominal and the
hot-day envelope trajectories.

\subsection{Stability, mass and payload}

Barrowman's method, the standard linear method also used by OpenRocket, gives the nose
$C_{N\alpha}=2$ at $x_{cp}=L-V/A_b$ and the assumed fins $C_{N\alpha,f}=7.4$. The vehicle CP is the
$C_{N\alpha}$-weighted mean of the components, and the static margin is $(x_{cp} - x_{cg})/D$.
Positions are measured from the aft end, so that fins and body stay fixed when the nose length
changes, and the CG shift from the nose-shell mass is included. The shell mass is the wetted area
times the Firehorn as-built areal density (\SI{0.98}{kg/m^2}: \SI{500}{g} for the \SI{960}{mm},
\O\SI{240}{mm} nose). The 3U fit is the length of curved nose over which $r\geq\SI{77}{mm}$.
