\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Nose wave drag}

Figure~\ref{fig:cdw}a gives the wave drag of the seven profiles at $L/D=4$ and
Figure~\ref{fig:cp} the wall pressure coefficient $C_p = (p-p_\infty)/q_\infty$ behind it. By
Equation~\eqref{eq:cdw}, regions with $C_p > 0$ add drag and regions with $C_p < 0$ reduce it.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Von K\'arm\'an and power $n=\tfrac12$ are the two low-drag profiles}, within a few
per cent of each other: Von K\'arm\'an is lower at $M \geq 1.3$, power $\tfrac12$ at $M \leq 1.2$.
The tangent profiles expand continuously and their wall pressure falls below ambient over the aft
third, which partly cancels the overpressure near the tip. The power-$\tfrac12$ nose concentrates
its overpressure in a short spike at its blunt tip and stays just above ambient behind it.
\item \textbf{Ogive, parabolic and LV-Haack} carry 13--22\,\% more wave drag: their fuller forebody
compresses the flow harder near the tip (higher $C_p$ between $x/L \approx 0.1$ and 0.4,
Figure~\ref{fig:cp}b).
\item \textbf{The cone is the worst} at every Mach number, and its drag rises steeply towards Mach 1:
its constant surface angle keeps the conical overpressure all the way to the shoulder.
\item \textbf{Fineness.} $C_{D,\rm w}$ falls as $1/(L/D)^2$ for every profile, the slope of
slender-body theory (Figure~\ref{fig:cdw}b). For Von K\'arm\'an the Euler level is about 15\,\%
below the linear-theory value, so linear theory gives the trend but not the level.
\end{itemize}

Length is as powerful as shape. Going from $L/D = 4$ to 4.5 lowers the Von K\'arm\'an wave drag at
$M = 1.4$ by 19\,\% (0.0526 to 0.0425), close to the factor $(4/4.5)^2 = 0.79$ of
Equation~\eqref{eq:fineness}, while at equal length the ogive carries 21\,\% more than the
Von K\'arm\'an nose. The design question is therefore how to combine the best profile with the
longest nose the vehicle can accommodate.

\subsection{Flight performance}

Weisshorn burns out at \SI{9.1}{s} near \SI{2.2}{km} at $M_{\max}=1.47$ (nominal) or 1.56
(envelope), spends \SI{16}{s} above $M = 0.8$ and \SI{10}{s} above Mach 1, and crosses the
transonic band twice (Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory}a). The drag impulse $\int D\,\mathrm dt$, the
momentum lost to drag, is spent mostly between $M = 0.8$ and 1.5 (Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory}b),
where the dynamic pressure is highest and where the nose wave drag acts. The apogee sensitivities
around the baseline are \textbf{\SI{-100}{m} per $\Delta C_D = 0.01$} and \textbf{\SI{-143}{m/kg}}.
A wave-drag difference only acts above $M = 0.8$ and is worth somewhat less: the ogive carries 0.009
more $C_{D,\rm w}$ than Von K\'arm\'an at $M = 1.4$ and $L/D = 4.5$, and loses \SI{79}{m} rather
than \SI{90}{m}. Skin friction dominates the vehicle drag at all speeds (Weisshorn is a
fineness-30 airframe), base drag is second, and the nose wave drag is 10--12\,\% of the total
above $M = 0.8$. This is why the nose shape moves the apogee by tens of metres rather than
kilometres, and why the length (wetted area) and shape effects are of the same order.

\subsection{Trade study}

Von K\'arm\'an still gives the highest apogee per millimetre of bay.
\item \textbf{Stability is not a discriminator}: all candidates change the static margin by less
than 0.1 cal (at most 0.16 cal over the whole design space, against the 2--4 cal target).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Robustness of the ranking}
\label{sec:robust}

The weakest parts of the chain are the transonic model, the drag of the rest of the vehicle and the
mass model. Figure~\ref{fig:robustness} recomputes the apogee of every shape at $L/D = 4.5$ with
the onset of the transonic drag rise moved to $M = 0.75$ and 0.85, the transonic wave drag scaled
by $\pm20\,\%$, the rest of the vehicle drag increased by 20\,\%, and the hot-day envelope with the
LVO masses, relative to Von K\'arm\'an in the same scenario:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Von K\'arm\'an flies highest in every scenario.} The power noses stay within
\SIrange{11}{31}{m} of it, the ogive, parabolic and LV-Haack noses are \SIrange{57}{98}{m} lower,
and the cone \SIrange{98}{156}{m} lower; no design crosses zero.
\item The spread between scenarios is smaller than the gap between the groups, so the selection does
not hinge on the transonic assumption. The hot-day envelope amplifies the differences; a draggier
vehicle shrinks them, because the nose is then a smaller share of the total.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tip bluntness}
\label{sec:bluntness}

A sharp tip cannot be made and chips first, so the apex is replaced by a sphere. On the
Von K\'arm\'an nose at $M=1.4$, a \SI{4.5}{mm} spherical tip ($r_n/R=0.05$) costs \BLUNTCOST{} in
wave drag (\BLUNTAPOA{} of apogee) and a \SI{9}{mm} tip ($r_n/R=0.1$) costs \BLUNTTEN{}
(\BLUNTAPOB{}). The cost grows quickly because the stagnation pressure at the centre of the sphere
($C_p \approx 1.5$ at $M = 1.4$, against about 0.04 averaged over a sharp nose) acts on a growing
area. A \SI{4.5}{mm} tip keeps the drag cost below 1\,\%.
