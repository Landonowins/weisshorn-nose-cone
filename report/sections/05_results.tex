\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Nose wave drag}

Figure~\ref{fig:cdw}a gives the wave drag of the seven profiles at $L/D=4$, Figure~\ref{fig:cp} the wall
pressure behind it ($C_p > 0$ adds drag, $C_p < 0$ reduces it, Equation~\eqref{eq:cdw}).
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Von K\'arm\'an and power $n=\tfrac12$ are the two low-drag profiles}, within a few per cent:
Von K\'arm\'an is lower at $M \geq 1.3$, power $\tfrac12$ at $M \leq 1.2$. The tangent profiles fall below
ambient pressure over the aft third; the power-$\tfrac12$ nose concentrates its overpressure in a spike at
its blunt tip.
\item \textbf{Ogive, parabolic and LV-Haack} carry 13--22\,\% more: their fuller forebody compresses the
flow harder near the tip (Figure~\ref{fig:cp}b).
\item \textbf{The cone is the worst} at every Mach number: it keeps the conical overpressure to the
shoulder.
\item \textbf{Fineness.} $C_{D,\rm w}$ falls as $1/(L/D)^2$ for every profile (Figure~\ref{fig:cdw}b); for
Von K\'arm\'an the Euler level is about 15\,\% below linear theory.
\end{itemize}

Length is as powerful as shape: from $L/D = 4$ to 4.5 the Von K\'arm\'an wave drag at $M = 1.4$ falls by
19\,\% (0.0526 to 0.0425, close to $(4/4.5)^2 = 0.79$), while at equal length the ogive carries 21\,\%
more than Von K\'arm\'an. The question is how to combine the best profile with the longest nose the
vehicle can take.

\subsection{Flight performance}

Weisshorn burns out at \SI{9.1}{s} near \SI{2.2}{km} at $M_{\max}=1.47$ (nominal) or 1.56 (envelope),
spends \SI{16}{s} above $M = 0.8$ and \SI{10}{s} above Mach 1 (Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory}a). The drag
impulse is spent mostly between $M = 0.8$ and 1.5 (Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory}b), where the nose wave drag
acts. Apogee sensitivities: \textbf{\SI{-100}{m} per $\Delta C_D = 0.01$} and \textbf{\SI{-143}{m/kg}}; a
wave-drag difference acts only above $M = 0.8$ and is worth less (the ogive's $+0.009$ in $C_{D,\rm w}$
costs \SI{79}{m}, not \SI{90}{m}). Skin friction dominates the vehicle drag, base drag is second, and the
nose wave drag is 10--12\,\% of the total above $M = 0.8$: the nose shape moves the apogee by tens of
metres, and length and shape effects are of the same order.

\subsection{Trade study}
\label{sec:trade}

Figure~\ref{fig:trade} and Table~\ref{tab:trade} give, for every profile and fineness, the apogee change
(a full trajectory with the wave drag, length, wetted area and mass of that nose), the 3U length inside
and the static-margin change. The curves rise with fineness as the wave drag falls as $1/(L/D)^2$, and
flatten as friction and mass grow with $L$.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Von K\'arm\'an, power $\tfrac12$ and power $\tfrac34$ lead at every length} within about
\SI{30}{m}: power $\tfrac12$ at $L/D\leq3.5$, Von K\'arm\'an at $L/D = 4$--4.5, power $\tfrac34$ at
$L/D=5$. At $L/D=4$, Von K\'arm\'an beats the tangent ogive by \TRADEOGIVE{} and the power-$\tfrac12$ by
\TRADEPOWER{}.
\item \textbf{Length pays, with diminishing returns}: \TRADELEN{} per extra \SI{90}{mm} around
$L/D = 4$--4.5.
\item \textbf{Fuller noses house more payload}: at $L/D=4$ ogive and LV-Haack fit \SIrange{275}{284}{mm}
of the 3U, Von K\'arm\'an \SI{226}{mm}; per millimetre of nose bay, Von K\'arm\'an still gives the highest
apogee.
\item \textbf{Stability is not a discriminator}: all candidates change the static margin by less than
0.1 cal (0.16 cal at most over the design space, against the 2--4 cal target).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Robustness of the ranking}
\label{sec:robust}

Figure~\ref{fig:robustness} recomputes every shape at $L/D = 4.5$ with the transonic onset at $M = 0.75$
and 0.85, the transonic wave drag $\pm20\,\%$, the rest of the vehicle drag $+20\,\%$, and the hot-day
envelope, relative to Von K\'arm\'an:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Von K\'arm\'an flies highest in every scenario.} The power noses stay within
\SIrange{11}{31}{m}, ogive, parabolic and LV-Haack are \SIrange{57}{98}{m} lower, the cone
\SIrange{98}{156}{m}; no design crosses zero.
\item The spread between scenarios is smaller than the gap between groups, so the selection does not
hinge on the transonic assumption. The hot day amplifies the differences; a draggier vehicle shrinks them.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tip bluntness}
\label{sec:bluntness}

On the Von K\'arm\'an nose at $M=1.4$, a \SI{4.5}{mm} spherical tip ($r_n/R=0.05$) costs \BLUNTCOST{} in
wave drag (\BLUNTAPOA{} of apogee), a \SI{9}{mm} tip \BLUNTTEN{} (\BLUNTAPOB{}): the stagnation pressure
($C_p \approx 1.5$, against about 0.04 averaged over a sharp nose) acts on a growing area. A \SI{4.5}{mm}
tip keeps the cost below 1\,\%.
