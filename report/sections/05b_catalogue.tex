\section{Design Catalogue}
\label{sec:catalogue}

This section presents the seven candidates as designs at the recommended length, $L/D = 4.5$
($L = \SI{810}{mm}$, $D = \SI{180}{mm}$), each with the \SI{60}{mm} bonded shoulder of the Firehorn
design. Figure~\ref{fig:gallery} shows them to scale with the 3U payload placed as far forward as
each profile allows, Figure~\ref{fig:fields} the computed flow around them at $M = 1.4$, and
Table~\ref{tab:catalogue} collects the numbers (wave drag from the direct Euler runs at
$L/D = 4.5$).

In Figure~\ref{fig:fields} the shock leans back at roughly the Mach angle ($45.6^\circ$,
Equation~\eqref{eq:mach}). On the cone and the power noses the expansion is concentrated in a fan at
the shoulder kink; on the tangent profiles it is spread over the aft part of the nose itself. The
more intense the red region in contact with the nose, and the less blue on it, the higher the wave
drag. The designs compare as follows.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Conical -- rejected.} Smallest volume (\SI{6.9}{L}), wetted area and shell
(\SI{286}{g}), and the simplest (developable) mould, but a $6.3^\circ$ shoulder kink. The conical
overpressure acts on the whole surface and all the turning happens in one expansion at the shoulder,
so it has the highest wave drag at every Mach number (46\,\% above Von K\'arm\'an at $M=1.4$). Only
\SI{117}{mm} of the 3U fits inside (bay \SI{1043}{mm}); $-\SI{132}{m}$ against Von K\'arm\'an.
\item \textbf{Tangent ogive -- acceptable alternative for volume-driven designs.} The classic
subsonic nose of Bella Lui~II and Nordend, with a $13^\circ$ tip semi-angle and a tangent shoulder.
Its fuller forebody compresses the flow harder near the tip: 21\,\% more wave drag than
Von K\'arm\'an at $M=1.4$. It packages well (\SI{309}{mm} of the 3U inside, bay \SI{870}{mm}) and
its single radius is the easiest smooth profile to make, but it costs \SI{79}{m} of apogee (about
\SI{0.55}{kg} of dry mass) for \SI{37}{mm} less bay.
\item \textbf{Parabolic ($K' = 1$) -- same as the ogive.} At equal length it is practically the
tangent ogive (wave drag within 0.4\,\%). Crowell's chart rates it best in the high subsonic range
(Table~\ref{tab:crowell}), which is irrelevant for a vehicle whose drag losses lie at $M = 0.8$--1.5.
\item \textbf{Power $n = \tfrac12$ (Wildhorn, Firehorn) -- the choice for a short nose.} Same volume
as Von K\'arm\'an (\SI{10.3}{L}) but further forward, and a $3.2^\circ$ shoulder kink. The blunt apex
detaches the bow shock and creates the only closed sonic line of Figure~\ref{fig:fields}; behind
this spike the wall pressure stays slightly above ambient. Its wave drag is 3--10\,\% above
Von K\'arm\'an at $L/D=4.5$, but it is the lowest of all at $L/D = 3$ and at $M \leq 1.1$ for
$L/D = 4$. \SI{217}{mm} of the 3U fits inside (bay \SI{943}{mm}); it flies \SI{22}{m} below
Von K\'arm\'an (about \SI{0.15}{kg}) and within \SI{31}{m} of it in every robustness scenario.
\item \textbf{Power $n = \tfrac34$ -- not selected.} Between the cone and the $\tfrac12$-power nose:
pointed tip, slender body, small volume (\SI{8.2}{L}), $4.8^\circ$ kink. Its wave drag falls fastest
with Mach number ($0.0513 \rightarrow 0.0432$ from $M = 1.2$ to 1.6) and its low wetted area saves
friction, so it ends only \SI{18}{m} below Von K\'arm\'an. It has the worst packaging of the smooth
shapes (\SI{152}{mm} of the 3U inside, bay \SI{1008}{mm}) and would only make sense on a faster
vehicle ($M_{\max} \approx 2$) with the payload outside the nose.
\item \textbf{LV-Haack ($C = \tfrac13$) -- the choice if payload volume were the driver.} The minimum
wave drag for a given length \emph{and volume}: the fullest design (\SI{11.6}{L}), tangent shoulder,
13--19\,\% more wave drag than Von K\'arm\'an over $M = 1.2$--1.6. It has the best payload room
(\SI{320}{mm} of the 3U inside, bay \SI{870}{mm}), the heaviest shell (\SI{376}{g}) and the largest
static-margin reduction ($-0.08$ cal); it costs \SI{69}{m}, marginally better than the ogive for
the same bay.
\item \textbf{Von K\'arm\'an ($C = 0$) -- recommended.} The minimum wave drag for a given length
\emph{and base area}: not constrained in volume, it stays thin near the tip and spreads the area
growth along the length. \SI{10.3}{L}, tangent shoulder, $x_{cp}/L = 0.5$. The compression is weak
and confined to the first few per cent of the length, and the wall pressure is below ambient over
the aft third. It has the lowest wave drag at every computed Mach number at $L/D = 4.5$ (0.0437,
0.0425 and 0.0414 at $M = 1.2$, 1.4 and 1.6); the \SI{4.5}{mm} tip adds $+0.5$ to $+1.4\,\%$.
\SI{254}{mm} of the 3U fits inside (bay \SI{907}{mm}), the tangent shoulder gives a clean bond line,
and it flies highest in all seven robustness scenarios.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Literature cross-check}
\label{sec:crowell}

The drag-rating chart compiled by Crowell (reproduced on Wikipedia's ``Nose cone design'' page,
CC BY-SA 4.0, and used in the Firehorn nosecone DJF) condenses published drag comparisons into four
qualitative classes and gives an independent, if coarse, check (Table~\ref{tab:crowell}; the chart
does not state the fineness ratio of its data).

Both sources rate the ogive and the cone worst at transonic and low supersonic speeds and the
Von K\'arm\'an, Haack and power-law noses as the good ones. Around $M \approx 1.5$ the chart prefers
the $\tfrac12$-power nose; the Euler results reproduce that at $L/D = 3$ but not at $L/D \geq 4$,
where Von K\'arm\'an is lower at every Mach number. The ranking depends on fineness, so the
recommendation rests on the computed ranking at the actual design fineness.
