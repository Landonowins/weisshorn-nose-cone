\section{Running Studies}
\label{sec:running}

\subsection{Commands}

Everything goes through one entry point, \texttt{python -m nosecone <command>} (Table~\ref{tab:commands};
\texttt{python -m nosecone <command> -{}-help} lists the options of each command).

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Commands. Typical run times on a laptop with the Weisshorn database.}
\label{tab:commands}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4.4cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{9.0cm}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{2.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
Command & What it does & Time \\
\midrule
\texttt{run CONFIG} & The whole study: sweep, sensitivities, robustness, heating, CAD, plots, tables. Options:
\texttt{-{}-plots trade,cdw}, \texttt{-{}-no-plots}, \texttt{-{}-latex}, \texttt{-{}-output DIR}, \texttt{-{}-quiet} & 1--5 min \\
\texttt{export CONFIG} & Only the CAD files of the selected design (CSV, STL, STEP) & seconds \\
\texttt{validate CONFIG} & Check the file; \texttt{-{}-show} prints all values used & instant \\
\texttt{describe-config} & Every parameter with unit, range and default (\texttt{-{}-basic}: hide advanced) & instant \\
\texttt{profiles} & Profile families and built-in profiles & instant \\
\texttt{cfd CONFIG} & Run the CFD studies of \key{cfd.studies} (\texttt{-{}-study NAME}, \texttt{-{}-workers N}, \texttt{-{}-dry-run}) & 3--10 min per geometry \\
\texttt{rebuild-database CONFIG} & Recompute the database CSV from the CFD wall-pressure files & 1 min \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

A run prints its progress and writes it to \texttt{run.log}. When it finishes, start with \texttt{summary.md}.

\subsection{Worked example 1: a single design}
\label{sec:single}

\emph{Question: how does a Von K\'arm\'an nose of \SI{810}{mm} ($L/D = 4.5$) with a \SI{4.5}{mm} aluminium tip
perform on Weisshorn?} The file \texttt{configs/example\_single\_design.yaml} inherits the whole vehicle from
\texttt{weisshorn.yaml} and only restricts the sweep to that one nose:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=yaml]
base: weisshorn.yaml
project:
  name: Weisshorn single design
  output_dir: ../output/example_single_design
nose:
  profiles: [vonkarman]          # the "sweep" is a single nose...
  fineness: [4.5]                # ...at a single fineness
  design_profile: vonkarman
  design_fineness: 4.5
  design_tip_radius_ratio: 0.05  # 4.5 mm spherical tip (r_n / R)
robustness: {enabled: false}     # nothing to rank with one profile
trade_matrix: {enabled: false}
cad: {candidates: false}         # only the design
output:
  plots: [profiles, trajectory, breakdown, bluntness, thermal, final]
\end{lstlisting}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
python -m nosecone run configs/example_single_design.yaml
\end{lstlisting}
The console shows each step:
\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
CFD database: 126 points (euler_results.csv)
Drag factor k = 0.5601 (calibrated: baseline reaches 9000 m)
Sweep: 1 profiles x 1 fineness x 1 tip radii
  sensitivities: -142.5 m/kg, -99.8 m per 0.01 of C_D
Trajectories: M_max 1.47 nominal, 1.56 envelope; apogee 9000 m
Heating [degC] nominal/envelope: T0 119/156, metal tip 51/72, printed tip 104/138, shell inner 77/104
Tip r_n/R = 0.05: wave drag +0.6 %, apogee -2 m
CAD export:
  STL nose_vonkarman_LD4.5_rn4.5mm_FINAL.stl: 122624 triangles, open edges 0, volume 11.828 L
  STEP nose_vonkarman_LD4.5_rn4.5mm_FINAL.step: 1 solid, volume 11.842 L
\end{lstlisting}
and \texttt{summary.md} gives the answer (some columns left out here):
\begin{lstlisting}[language={},basicstyle=\ttfamily\scriptsize]
| shape     | fineness | cd_w_12 | cd_w_14 | cd_w_16 | apogee | d_apogee | mass_g | l3U_mm | bay_mm | dSM_cal |
| vonkarman | 4.5      | 0.0437  | 0.0425  | 0.0414  | 9060   | +60      | 354    | 254    | 907    | -0.036  |
\end{lstlisting}
\textbf{Reading it.} The calibration factor $k = 0.56$ scales the drag of the rest of the vehicle so that the
\emph{baseline} nose (Von K\'arm\'an at $L/D = 4$, the length of the LVS) reaches the 9~km of the LVO. With that
vehicle, the \SI{810}{mm} nose flies \SI{60}{m} higher than the baseline (\texttt{d\_apogee}), weighs \SI{354}{g},
houses \SI{254}{mm} of the 3U payload in its curved part and lowers the static margin by 0.036 calibre. On a
hot day the aluminium tip stays at \SI{72}{\celsius} while a printed PET-CF tip would reach
\SI{138}{\celsius} (Figure~\ref{fig:ex1}b). The files to give to Structure are in \texttt{cad/}.

\subsection{Worked example 2: a parameter sweep}
\label{sec:sweep}

\emph{Question: among the power-$\tfrac12$ (Firehorn), LV-Haack and Von K\'arm\'an profiles, which is best on
Weisshorn, and how long should the nose be?} Any parameter given as a list in \key{nose.profiles},
\key{nose.fineness} or \key{nose.tip\_radius\_ratios} is swept: here 3 profiles $\times$ 4 fineness ratios.
\begin{lstlisting}[language=yaml]
base: weisshorn.yaml
project:
  name: Weisshorn sweep
  output_dir: ../output/example_sweep
nose:
  profiles: [power050, lvhaack, vonkarman]
  fineness: [3.5, 4.0, 4.5, 5.0]
output:
  plots: [cdw, trade, robustness, gallery, schematics]
\end{lstlisting}
All twelve noses are compared in one table, \texttt{summary.md} (sorted by apogee; here the first rows):
\begin{lstlisting}[language={},basicstyle=\ttfamily\scriptsize]
| shape     | fineness | cd_w_14 | apogee | d_apogee | mass_g | l3U_mm | bay_mm | dSM_cal |
| vonkarman | 5        | 0.0350  | 9099   | +99      | 386    | 282    | 968    | -0.071  |
| power050  | 5        | 0.0387  | 9075   | +75      | 394    | 241    | 1009   | -0.067  |
| vonkarman | 4.5      | 0.0425  | 9060   | +60      | 354    | 254    | 907    | -0.036  |
| power050  | 4.5      | 0.0457  | 9038   | +38      | 361    | 217    | 943    | -0.032  |
| lvhaack   | 5        | 0.0410  | 9036   | +36      | 411    | 355    | 960    | -0.125  |
| ...
\end{lstlisting}
and on the same plots (Figure~\ref{fig:ex2}). \textbf{Reading it.} From $L/D = 4$ upwards the Von K\'arm\'an nose
flies highest (8 to 24~m above the power-$\tfrac12$ nose); at $L/D = 3.5$ the power-$\tfrac12$ nose is slightly
better (6~m). The LV-Haack nose is 63--81~m lower but houses more of the payload (\texttt{l3U\_mm}). Each extra
\SI{90}{mm} of length ($\Delta L/D = 0.5$) is worth less apogee than the previous one (+85, +60, +39~m for the
Von K\'arm\'an nose). The robustness plot shows that, at $L/D = 4.5$, the ranking does not change under any of the
seven modelling scenarios, so the choice does not depend on the uncertain transonic model. Whether to take
$L/D = 4.5$ or 5 is then a vehicle-length question (\texttt{bay\_mm}), not an aerodynamic one.

\subsection{The full Weisshorn study}

\texttt{configs/weisshorn.yaml} is the configuration of the design report: seven profiles $\times$ five fineness
ratios, all plots, and the LaTeX tables and macros of the report (\key{output.latex: true}, written to
\texttt{output/weisshorn/latex/}). \texttt{python tests/verify\_reference.py} checks that these outputs are still
identical to the report.

\subsection{Another rocket}
\label{sec:newrocket}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item Copy \texttt{configs/example\_new\_rocket.yaml} (a complete example for a 152~mm rocket) to
\texttt{configs/my\_rocket.yaml} and set \key{project.name} and \key{project.output\_dir}.
\item Fill in \key{vehicle}, \key{engine}, \key{fins} and \key{launch} from your LVO/LVS.
\item \textbf{Calibration.} If you trust a reference apogee for a reference nose (e.g.\ from RocketPy or a
previous flight), keep \key{flight.calibrate: true} and set \key{flight.target\_apogee},
\key{nose.baseline\_profile} and \key{nose.baseline\_fineness}. Otherwise set \key{flight.calibrate: false}: the
empirical drag build-up is used as is ($k = 1$); apogees are then less accurate, but the comparison between noses
remains meaningful.
\item Choose the noses: \key{nose.profiles}, \key{nose.fineness}, and the selected design (\key{design\_*}).
\item Set or disable the payload (\key{payload.enabled}), check the structure and heating inputs, and adapt
\key{trade\_matrix.manufacture} and \key{heritage} to your team.
\item \texttt{python -m nosecone validate configs/my\_rocket.yaml}, then \texttt{run}.
\end{enumerate}
The CFD database is non-dimensional (lengths scaled by the base radius), so it holds for any diameter: only
profiles, fineness ratios or tip radii outside it require new CFD.

\subsection{New CFD cases}
\label{sec:newcfd}

There are two ways to add wave-drag data.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{On demand}: \key{aerodynamics.compute\_missing: true}. Before the sweep, the tool solves every
profile / tip radius that has no data, at the smallest and largest fineness of the sweep and at the design
fineness, for \key{aerodynamics.missing\_machs}, in parallel (\key{cfd.workers}). Set
\key{aerodynamics.user\_database} to keep these results in a separate CSV.
\item \textbf{Planned studies}: list them in \key{cfd.studies}, run \texttt{python -m nosecone cfd CONFIG}
(add \texttt{-{}-dry-run} first to see the job list), then \texttt{python -m nosecone rebuild-database CONFIG}.
\end{itemize}
One Mach number takes 1--5~minutes on the medium grid ($160\times80$ cells), more near $M = 1.1$; a geometry with
four Mach numbers takes 5--10~minutes per CPU core. The solver is deterministic: the same case gives the same result
to the last bit.
