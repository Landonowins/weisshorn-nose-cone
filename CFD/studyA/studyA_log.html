Nose cone study A: LV-Haack, 2D axisymmetric RANS

LV-Haack nose with a 561 mm curved part and an 89 mm coupler, D = 180 mm, tip radius 4.5 mm, at zero incidence. Steady RANS runs at M = 1.47, 1.2 and 0.95 on the nominal Weisshorn trajectory, in Ansys Fluent 2024 R2. This page records every step from the CAD file to the converged drag, with the formula behind each input, so the runs can be reproduced and written up by someone who has not touched the simulation.

Building the domain (Workbench 2024 R2 + Discovery)

  1. Fluid Flow (Fluent) system; Geometry cell → Properties → Analysis Type = 2D, set before importing. Without it Fluent does not offer the axisymmetric option.
  2. Import the STEP and open it in Discovery.
  3. Pull the coupler end disc by +1800 mm, to x = 2447.65 mm, so the cylinder runs through to the outlet.
  4. Sketch in the XY plane a rectangle from (2447.65, 0) to (−3600, 3600) mm.
  5. Combine → Subtract from target: rectangle as target, solid as cutter. Delete the solid and any leftover piece inside the body.
  6. Result: one surface, y ≥ 0, with the nose profile cut out of its lower edge. The nose and cylinder edges are already split at the shoulder because they are separate faces in the STEP.

Why the domain looks like this

  • Axisymmetric 2D. At zero incidence the flow is identical in every meridional plane, so the half-plane y ≥ 0 describes it. Fluent revolves that plane about the x axis and integrates wall forces over the full 360°, so forces and CD are those of the full body.
  • Far field 20D away. 3.6 m upstream of the tip and 3.6 m radially (D = 0.18 m), so the far-field boundaries sit where the flow is undisturbed.
  • Outlet 10.5D behind the shoulder. x = 2.448 m, 1.889 m behind the shoulder, so the shock system and boundary layer leave the domain cleanly.
  • Cylinder through the outlet. A body that ends inside the domain has a blunt base with a separated, unsteady wake that a steady solver cannot converge, and at subsonic speed the base pressure would act upstream on the nose. A cylinder running out of the domain gives clean forebody numbers, the same as the team's Euler nose-plus-cylinder setup. Its length is set by the domain, not by the real rocket.

Turbulence at the inlet, far field and outlet

Specified as turbulence intensity I = 1 % and viscosity ratio μt/μ = 10 on all three. Fluent converts them to the k-ω variables:

k = 3/2 · (I V)²
ω = ρ k / (μ · μt/μ)
QuantityM = 0.95M = 1.20M = 1.47
I V (velocity fluctuation)3.20 m/s4.01 m/s4.88 m/s
k15.3 m²/s²24.2 m²/s²35.7 m²/s²
ω9.76·10⁴ s⁻¹1.47·10⁵ s⁻¹2.05·10⁵ s⁻¹
μt = ρ k / ω1.76·10⁻⁴ Pa s1.74·10⁻⁴ Pa s1.72·10⁻⁴ Pa s
  • Why these values. 1 % and 10 are common, conservative choices for external aerodynamics in calm air. The workflow does not prescribe them; they were chosen for this study. A sensitivity check is listed under open items.
  • Why not Fluent's defaults. New boundaries default to k = 1 m²/s² and ω = 1 s⁻¹. That gives μt = ρ k / ω ≈ 1 Pa s, about 57 000–64 000 times the laminar viscosity, which would swamp the boundary layer. It was caught before the first run and replaced on all three boundaries.

Reference values and drag coefficient

Aref = π D² / 4 = π × 0.18² / 4 = 0.025447 m² (entered as 0.02545)
CD = Fx / (q Aref), q = ½ ρ∞ V∞²
ρ, V, T and p are computed by Fluent from inlet for each Mach number (Report → Reference Values → Compute from inlet). The reference length 0.18 m only affects moment coefficients. Fx is split by Fluent into the pressure integral and the wall shear-stress integral.

Note on the outlet backflow temperature

The outlet's backflow total temperature was set to the free-stream static temperature T, not to the free-stream total temperature T0 (332.9 / 358.5 / 392.0 K). The value is only used where flow re-enters the domain through the outlet. Using T0 would be more consistent; see open items.

Convergence criterion (from the workflow; residuals alone do not count):
Δ = (max CD − min CD) / mean CD over the last 500 iterations < 0.1 %
  • Viscous heating. The density-based solver always includes viscous dissipation in the energy equation; the pressure-based solver needs it switched on (Viscous model → Options). Without it the adiabatic wall would stay too cold at M ≈ 1 and the skin friction would be off.
  • Residual stopping off for the pressure-based run. Its residuals are normalised differently and dropped below Fluent's default 10⁻³ immediately, which stopped the run after 7 iterations. All residual criteria were set to 10⁻¹² so only the CD criterion decides.
  • Why a different solver at M = 0.95. The density-based solver did not settle at this Mach number (section 5). The pressure-based coupled solver converged without oscillation.

Comparison with the team's Euler results

The Euler study f650 in data/euler_results.csv ran the same LV-Haack family at fineness 2.5 and 3.5 (sharp tip) to bracket this design (fineness 3.117). Euler gives wave drag only, so it compares with Fluent's pressure CD. Interpolated to fineness 3.117, both linearly in L/D and in 1/(L/D)², since wave drag scales roughly with 1/(L/D)²:

Fluent falls inside both interpolation ranges (−3 % to +4 %), and Euler also shows the slight rise from M = 1.2 to 1.47. Remaining differences are expected: the real nose has a 4.5 mm blunt tip, and RANS includes the boundary layer's displacement effect.

Consistency across the sweep

  • Viscous CD rises smoothly as Mach drops: 0.0207 → 0.0224 → 0.0248, following the lower Reynolds number and lower dynamic pressure.
  • Cylinder friction is consistent: viscous CD on wall_body 0.0745 (M 1.47), 0.0824 (M 1.2), 0.0835 (M 0.95). It is excluded from the nose result.
  • y+ < 1 everywhere on both walls at all three Mach numbers.
Step 7

7 Problems met and how they were solved

In the order they happened, over the two days.

  1. No mesh in the first Fluent session

    The 2026 R1 session had no zones and no mesh existed on disk. The 2D domain was built in Discovery from the LV-Haack STEP.

  2. Workflow PDF describes a different nose

    The PDF is written for the 810 mm Von Kármán. Study A uses the 561 mm LV-Haack; the solver settings and free-stream table are the same.

  3. version change

    Ansys Meshing 2026 R1 would not start

    "Object reference not set to an instance of an object" at start-up. Switched to Ansys 2024 R2 as the TAs advised and rebuilt the geometry from the STEP.

  4. Geometry not attached as 2D after a restart

    The project had never been saved, so the geometry lived in a Temp folder. It was copied into the repository and reattached in a new 2D system.

  5. Leftover rocket body and quad mesher failure

    The inner body was suppressed. The failure was isolated with a coarse test mesh, then the full settings were restored step by step.

  6. Max skewness 0.985 along wall_body

    38 bad cells at the quad/triangle transition near the outlet. Reducing the wall_body bias from 10 to 3 brought the maximum to 0.92.

  7. No server command in the Fluent 2024 R2 console

    After turning on beta feature access, server/start-server worked.

  8. Zone name typo

    wal_nose renamed to wall_nose.

  9. physics

    Free-stream turbulence at Fluent's defaults

    k = 1, ω = 1 gives an eddy viscosity about 60 000 times laminar. Set to intensity 1 % and viscosity ratio 10 on inlet, farfield and outlet.

  10. Outlet broken by misaligned console prompts

    Pressure, multiplier, average-pressure and target-mass-flow settings ended up wrong, and empty profiles stopped the first iteration. Fixed in the GUI and checked from the written settings file.

  11. Iterate call hung

    Fluent waited at "Number of iterations". Iterations were run with /solve/iterate n from the console.

  12. workbench

    Workbench settings prompt and save error

    After each setting change Workbench asked whether to keep it, and the console answers failed; saving the Fluent state also failed. From day 2 Fluent ran standalone, reading studyA_M1.47.cas.h5.

  13. Console boundary commands unreliable

    The far-field command reset the turbulence method to K and Omega, and p in the outlet's keyword menu is the profile multiplier, not the pressure. All boundary values were then entered in the GUI and each change was checked with /file/write-bc.

  14. Typo in the far-field pressure

    9066 Pa instead of 90 666 Pa at M = 0.95, caught by the settings-file check before running.

  15. Restart of M = 1.2 from the M = 1.47 solution went unstable

    CD barely moved for 180 iterations, then residuals grew fast. Each Mach number now starts from a fresh hybrid initialisation, which also resets the iteration count and plots.

  16. Rising residuals at M = 1.2

    Continuity rose from 1.3·10⁻³ to 8·10⁻³. Lowering the Courant number from 5 to 3 held it flat; CD was unaffected.

  17. solver change

    Density-based solver would not converge at M = 0.95

    CD cycled between 0.009 and 0.053. A non-reflecting outlet reduced it to 0.030–0.040, and Courant 1 did not remove it. The pressure-based coupled solver converged cleanly.

  18. Pressure-based run stopped after 7 iterations

    Fluent reported "solution is converged" against its default residual criteria. All residual criteria were set to 10⁻¹².

  19. Viscous heating has no console command here

    Ticked in the GUI (Viscous model → Options) and confirmed in the settings file. Compressibility effects were left off.

Step 8

8 Open items and limitations

check

Solver cross-check. M = 0.95 used the pressure-based solver, the other two the density-based solver. Re-running M = 1.2 with the pressure-based solver (about 1200 iterations) would show the solver choice does not shift the result.

check

Outlet backflow temperature. Set to the static T instead of the total T0. It only acts on reversed flow at the outlet, but the report should state it or the runs should be repeated with T0.

next

Mesh study at M = 1.47: coarse, medium (this mesh) and fine, about ×1.5 per direction each step, plus one or two pressure-gradient adaptions at the shock.

option

Turbulence boundary sensitivity. 1 % / 10 was chosen, not prescribed. A run at 0.1 % / 1 would show how much the viscous part depends on it.

later

3D half-model at 5° incidence for CN α and the centre of pressure, as in section 3 of the workflow.
